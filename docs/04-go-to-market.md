# Go-to-Market Strategy: Finding Your First 1,000 Families

You don't need a marketing budget to find your first 1,000 families. You need **precision, hustle, and an extraordinary product** for a specific community.

---

## The GTM Philosophy

Don't try to reach all parents everywhere. Instead:

**Go deep in a few communities before going wide.**

A parenting app with 50 families from the same school is 10x more valuable (to those families) than an app with 500 families from 100 different neighborhoods. Network density creates value. Density requires focus.

Your first 1,000 families should all be within 2–3 zip codes or 2–3 schools. Then expand.

---

## Phase 0 GTM: Waitlist (Weeks 1–8)

### The Landing Page
Before you have a product, you need a waitlist. The landing page should:
- Articulate the vision in 2 sentences (*"More wonder, less logistics. An AI that knows your family."*)
- Show what the product will do (3–4 benefits, not features)
- Have ONE call to action: enter your email
- Include social proof as you get it: *"47 parents from [City] already signed up"*

Build this in Framer or Webflow. No engineers needed. Takes 1 day.

### Waitlist Building Tactics
1. **Your personal network first**: Text every parent you know. Be personal, not broadcast.
2. **Parent Facebook groups**: Join the 3–5 biggest local parenting groups. Post authentically about the problem you're solving, not the product. *"I'm working on something to help parents find trusted activity recommendations from people they know — would love to hear your biggest frustrations with how you find activities for your kids."*
3. **Nextdoor**: Same approach, hyper-local.
4. **School newsletters**: Ask principals and parent association chairs if you can include a link in the school newsletter. Frame it as a resource for parents.
5. **Parenting podcasts**: Reach out to small-to-mid parenting podcasts (5K–50K listeners). Many will do an interview in exchange for a mention.
6. **Instagram/TikTok content**: Start creating content around the *problem* (parenting logistics overwhelm), not the solution. Build an audience before you have a product.

**Waitlist goal: 500–1,000 emails before launch**

## Phase 1 GTM: Community Launch (Months 2–5)

### The Anchor School Strategy

**How it works:**
1. Pick 3 schools in your launch city
2. Find 1 well-connected parent champion at each school
3. Offer them: free premium access for life + founding member status + direct line to you
4. They do a personal invite campaign within their school network
5. You support them: show up at school events, provide talking points, respond to every piece of feedback immediately
6. Goal: 100–150 families per school before expanding

**How to find champion parents:**
- School Parent Association chairs (they already have the trust network)
- The parent who runs the neighborhood Facebook group
- The "connector" parent everyone knows
- Parents who are vocal about this problem on social media

**What makes a great champion parent:**
- Well-connected within the school community
- Genuinely feels the pain of the problem
- Willing to be vocal about things they love
- Not necessarily the most popular parent — the most *trusted* parent

### Your Job in Phase 1 GTM
For the first 500 families, YOU are the customer success team, the community manager, and the sales team.

- **Do unscalable things**: Call new signups. Text champion parents. Show up at school events.
- **Create exclusivity**: *"We're launching in [neighborhood] first — you're in the founding community."*
- **Generate FOMO**: When one school is active and happy, neighboring schools will notice.
- **Fix things in public**: When a parent posts feedback in your community group, respond immediately and fix it. Nothing builds trust faster.

### Content Strategy (Runs Throughout)

**The goal**: Become the trusted voice on the intersection of parenting + local discovery.

**What to create:**
- *"Hidden Gem" series*: Weekly post about an underrated kids' activity in your city. Zero Joyful branding — just genuinely helpful content.
- *Parent spotlights*: Interview well-connected parents about how they find activities for their kids. This builds relationships and content simultaneously.
- *"This week in [city] for families"*: A weekly roundup of activities and events. Builds the habit of checking in with you.
- *"How we raised X"*: Short, inspiring content about parenting philosophy. Builds the emotional brand.

**Channels in priority order for Phase 1:**
1. Instagram (visual, discoverable, high parent concentration)
2. Email newsletter (highest engagement, owned audience)
3. TikTok (growing parenting community, high organic reach)
4. Nextdoor + local Facebook groups (hyperlocal, high trust)

**Content volume target**: 3–4 posts/week across channels. Quality over quantity.

## Phase 2 GTM: Expansion (Months 5–12)

### City-by-City Expansion Playbook
When you've hit 500 active families in City 1 with NPS > 50, start City 2.

The playbook for each new city:
1. Identify 3 anchor schools
2. Find champion parents
3. Seed the activity catalog (3–4 weeks of content research)
4. Soft launch with anchor communities
5. PR push (local city blog/magazine)
6. Paid acquisition begins (Instagram + Google)

**Expansion sequence**: Start with cities where you have personal connections or existing networks. Your first external city should be one where you can fly in and spend a week.

### PR Strategy
- **Local press first**: Parenting blogs, city magazines ("[City] Parent", local lifestyle publications). They are hungry for hyperlocal product stories.
- **National parenting press**: Once you have a compelling story (500+ families, NPS > 60, a great founder story), pitch to: Well Family (NYT), The Cut's parenting section, Romper, Parents Magazine, Today Parenting.
- **Founder story**: Your story as a product leader starting this company is compelling. Use it. *"I've been a product leader my whole career and I still couldn't figure out how to find a great coding class for my kid"* is a better story than a feature announcement.

### Referral Program (Launch with Phase 2)
*"Give a friend a free month of Premium. They join, you both get a free month."*

Simple, bilateral referral. The product does the acquisition work. Your job is to make the product worth talking about.

## The Anti-Pattern to Avoid

**Don't try to be everywhere before you're something somewhere.**

The fastest path to 10,000 families is:
1. Be extraordinary for 500 families in one community
2. Let those families tell their friends in adjacent communities
3. Repeat

The slowest path to 10,000 families is:
1. Launch to everyone in 5 cities simultaneously
2. Get 2,000 signups but only 200 active users because no community has density
3. NPS is 20 because recommendations don't work without network density
4. Churn faster than you acquire

Focus is not a limitation. Focus is your competitive advantage.
