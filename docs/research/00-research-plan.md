# Customer Discovery Research Plan

## Purpose

Before building anything, we need to deeply understand the jobs parents are hiring a tool like Joyful to do. This research plan ensures we build the right thing for the right person at the right moment — not what we assume they need.

The goal is not to validate our solution. The goal is to understand the problem so clearly that the right solution becomes obvious.

---

## Research Questions (What We Need to Learn)

### Primary
1. What triggers a parent to start searching for a new activity for their child?
2. Where do parents go today, and who do they trust for recommendations?
3. What makes a recommendation feel trustworthy vs. hollow?
4. What does the coordination process look like after a decision is made?
5. Where does the current process break down and create the most friction?

### Secondary
6. What does a parent feel when logistics overwhelm their time with their kids?
7. How do parents think about their child's development relative to activities?
8. What role does their social network play in activity decisions?
9. What would need to be true for a parent to trust an AI with their family's information?
10. What are parents already paying for that touches this problem space?

---

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Depth Interviews (Weeks 1–4)
**50 semi-structured interviews, 45–60 minutes each**

Semi-structured means you have a guide (see `01-interview-guide.md`) but you follow the energy — when a parent lights up or gets frustrated, you go deeper there rather than moving to the next question. The guide is a safety net, not a script.

**Format:** In-person coffee (ideal) or video call. Voice-only is fine. Never email or async — you miss the emotional signals.

**Recording:** Ask permission, then record with Otter.ai or similar. This frees you to listen instead of take notes.

### Phase 2: Diary Study (Weeks 3–5, running in parallel)
**10 parents, 1-week self-documented activity search**

Ask 10 of your interview participants to document the next time they search for a new activity for their child *as it happens*. Voice memo, text, or photo — whatever is easiest for them. You want raw, in-the-moment data, not recalled.

Prompt them: *"Next time you find yourself looking for something new for your kid, send me a quick voice memo walking me through what you're doing and what you're thinking. It can be 60 seconds."*

This captures behavior you can't get in an interview — the actual tools used, the actual people consulted, the actual moment of decision.

### Phase 3: Synthesis (Week 5–6)
See `04-synthesis-template.md` for the exact process.

## Who to Recruit

### Target Profile
**Primary:** Parents with children ages 4–14, living in Lafayette/Lamorinda, with household income $100K+, currently enrolling kids in 2+ organized activities per year.

**Secondary (for contrast):** Parents who have *stopped* enrolling kids in organized activities, or who rely entirely on school-based programs. Understanding why they opted out sharpens your understanding of the core pain.

### Recruit Across These Dimensions

| Dimension | Variation to Include |
|---|---|
| Number of kids | 1 kid / 2 kids / 3+ kids |
| Kids' ages | 4–6 / 7–10 / 11–14 |
| Household structure | Two-parent, same schedules / Two-parent, different schedules / Single parent |
| Activity intensity | Light (1–2 activities/year) / Moderate (3–5) / Heavy (6+) |
| Tech comfort | Early adopter / Average / Skeptical |
| Community integration | Very connected (PTA, sports boards) / Somewhat / Newly arrived |

**Why variation matters:** You don't want all your interviews to be the same person. The parent of a 5-year-old has a completely different discovery pattern than the parent of a 12-year-old. Single parents feel the coordination burden differently than couples. Capture the range.

### How to Find Participants

1. **Your personal network** — Start here. Text 10 parents you know today. "I'm doing research for a project. Can I buy you coffee and ask you about how your family finds activities for your kids? 45 minutes, no pitch."
2. **Lamorinda Moms & More (Facebook)** — Post: *"Working on a parenting project and want to hear from Lafayette parents. Anyone willing to share how your family finds kids' activities? 45-min coffee on me."*
3. **Burton Valley / Happy Valley school pickup** — If you have kids at these schools or know someone who does, show up and ask directly.
4. **Lafayette Community Center classes** — Parents dropping off kids have a few minutes. Get their number for a follow-up conversation.
5. **Nextdoor Lafayette** — Similar post to Lamorinda Moms.

### Incentive
For strangers (not your existing network): $25 Amazon gift card or a coffee on you. This is not about the money — it signals respect for their time.

## Interview Logistics

### Before Each Interview
- Review the guide (don't memorize it — just refresh)
- Set up Otter.ai to record
- Bring a notebook for your own emotional observations (what made them pause, get animated, go quiet)
- Confirm: name, number of kids + ages, how they heard about activities recently

### During Each Interview
- The best follow-up question is almost always: **"Tell me more about that."**
- When they describe a frustration, ask: **"What did you do?"** — you want the behavior, not just the feeling
- When they describe a solution, ask: **"How often does that actually work?"** — don't let them round up
- Never mention Joyful or your product concept until the very end, if at all
- If time allows: at minute 40, describe the core concept neutrally (see interview guide) and capture raw reaction

### After Each Interview
- Within 1 hour: Write your single most important insight from that conversation. Just 1–2 sentences. Do this while it's fresh.
- Complete the synthesis template for that interview (see `04-synthesis-template.md`)
- Note any quotes verbatim — these become your product copy

## What You'll Have at the End

1. **3–5 validated Jobs to Be Done** — the core things parents are hiring this product to do
2. **Customer journey map** — validated (not hypothesized) flow from trigger to outcome
3. **Hierarchy of pain** — which friction points are most acute and most frequent
4. **Trust model** — exactly who parents trust, why, and under what conditions
5. **Language map** — the exact words parents use to describe the problem (your future copy)
6. **Persona sketches** — 2–3 distinct parent archetypes who will be your primary users
7. **Feature priority signal** — what to build first based on what matters most
