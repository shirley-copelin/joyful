# Customer Journey Hypothesis Map

This is our *pre-research hypothesis* of how a parent moves from a trigger moment through to the outcome Joyful is trying to deliver. Every element here should be validated or corrected through customer discovery.

The journey covers **one episode**: a parent identifies a need, discovers an activity, coordinates participation, and reflects on the outcome.

---

## The Journey at a Glance

```
TRIGGER → SEARCH → EVALUATE → DECIDE → COORDINATE → EXPERIENCE → REFLECT
```

Each stage has: what the parent is doing, thinking, feeling, and what tools they're using today.

---

## Stage 1: Trigger
**What prompts the search to begin**

### Hypothesized Triggers (in rough frequency order)
| Trigger | Example | Season |
|---|---|---|
| **Seasonal transition** | Summer starting, school year starting, season ending | August, May, December |
| **Child expresses interest** | "Mom, can I try gymnastics?" | Any time |
| **Social signal** | Friend mentions their kid loves an activity | Any time |
| **Current activity ends** | Class finishes, season over | End of session |
| **Child seems restless / bored** | Parent senses kid needs something new | Any time |
| **Developmental milestone** | Child turns 5, gets "old enough" for something | Birthday-driven |
| **Schedule opens up** | A previous commitment drops, new slot available | Any time |

### Parent State at Trigger
- **Thinking:** "We should find something for [child] to do."
- **Feeling:** Mild pressure, slight anxiety, possibly excitement
- **Urgency:** Usually low-to-medium; rarely emergency

### What We Need to Validate
- [ ] Which triggers are most common for Lafayette families?
- [ ] How much advance notice do parents typically have before a season/session starts?
- [ ] Does trigger type affect how they search? (Child's request vs. parent-initiated may produce different behaviors)

## Stage 2: Search
**How they look for options**

### Hypothesized Search Behavior

**First move (hypothesis: ask someone they trust)**
- Text or call 1–3 trusted parent friends directly
- Post in Lamorinda Moms Facebook group
- Ask at school pickup
- Ask a pediatrician (for developmental questions)

**Second move (hypothesis: Google to fill gaps)**
- Google "[activity type] [age] Lafayette CA"
- Yelp search
- Browse activity provider websites directly

**Third move (hypothesis: follow up on leads)**
- Visit shortlisted websites
- Email or call to ask about availability
- Ask the trusted parent more follow-up questions

### Tools Used Today
| Tool | Used For | Key Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| iMessage / WhatsApp | Asking trusted parents | Doesn't scale; can't ask everyone |
| Lamorinda Moms FB group | Crowd recommendations | Noisy; unknown trust of recommenders |
| Google Search | Discovering providers | No trust signal; high volume of irrelevant results |
| Yelp | Reading reviews | Stale, impersonal, not parent-specific |
| Activity provider websites | Getting details | Fragmented; requires visiting many sites |
| School newsletter / email | Passive discovery | Low frequency; often missed |
| Nextdoor | Local recommendations | Mixed quality |

### Parent State During Search
- **Thinking:** "I don't know where to start" / "I wish I could ask everyone I know at once"
- **Feeling:** Mild frustration, time pressure, desire to get this done
- **Time spent (hypothesis):** 1–3 hours spread over 1–2 weeks

### The Trust Hierarchy (Hypothesis)
```
Most trusted
│  Close friend with a kid the same age and similar values
│  Well-connected parent they see regularly (PTA, sports sidelines)
│  Parent from relevant community (e.g., swim team parent about swim programs)
│  Parent in local Facebook group (named, identifiable)
│  Anonymous Yelp/Google review
Least trusted
```

### What We Need to Validate
- [ ] Is the first move really asking a trusted person, or is it Google first?
- [ ] How many people does a typical parent ask before feeling they have enough information?
- [ ] Does the trust hierarchy above reflect reality? Where does it break down?
- [ ] How often does search fail to produce a satisfying answer?

## Stage 3: Evaluate
**How they assess options and narrow down**

### Hypothesized Evaluation Criteria (in order)
1. **Trust source** — Did someone I trust recommend this specifically?
2. **Age/fit** — Is it right for this child at this age?
3. **Schedule fit** — Does it work with our calendar?
4. **Proximity** — Is it close enough to be sustainable?
5. **Price** — Does it fit our budget?
6. **Social fit** — Are kids my child knows doing it?
7. **Quality signals** — Reviews, reputation, how long they've been operating

### The Evaluation Conversation
Evaluation often involves a second conversation with the co-parent:
- "Should we sign Emma up for this?"
- "Can you handle pickup on Tuesdays?"
- "Is $200/month okay for this?"

This is a coordination point that often introduces delay.

### Parent State During Evaluation
- **Thinking:** "Is this actually right for my kid? Am I missing something better?"
- **Feeling:** Decision fatigue if too many options; anxiety about commitment
- **Time spent (hypothesis):** 30 minutes to 2 hours

### What We Need to Validate
- [ ] Does the evaluation criteria order above match what parents actually describe?
- [ ] How much does "a trusted person specifically recommended this for a child like mine" reduce evaluation time?
- [ ] How often does the co-parent introduce friction or delay at this stage?
- [ ] How much second-guessing happens? Do parents often wonder if they chose wrong?

## Stage 4: Decide + Sign Up
**The commitment moment**

### Hypothesized Decision Behavior
- Decision made verbally ("let's do it")
- One parent handles the signup (often on a provider's website)
- Credit card info entered, account created per provider
- Confirmation email arrives (often gets buried)
- Calendar entry added manually

### Friction Points (Hypothesis)
- Provider websites are often outdated or clunky
- Having to create a new account for each provider
- Waiting lists with no status visibility
- Confirmation processes that feel uncertain
- Co-parent not aware of what was booked

### Parent State at Decision
- **Thinking:** "Okay, we're doing this. Now I just need to actually sign up."
- **Feeling:** Relief at deciding; mild annoyance at the signup process

### What We Need to Validate
- [ ] How often does a decision fall through between "we should do this" and actual signup?
- [ ] What causes it to fall through? (Forgot, too busy, signup was too hard, changed mind)
- [ ] Do parents describe the signup process as painful, or is the pain entirely in discovery?

---

## Stage 5: Coordinate
**Making the logistics work**

### Hypothesized Coordination Activities
- Add to shared family calendar (or remind yourself to add it)
- Inform co-parent of the new commitment
- Arrange transportation (who drives, which days)
- Coordinate carpool with other families (often over text)
- Set reminders (often forgotten until too late)
- Pack appropriate gear/snacks

### The Carpool Problem (Hypothesis)
- Carpool coordination is an entirely separate text chain
- "Who can do Tuesdays?" sent to 3–5 families
- Multiple rounds of back-and-forth before a schedule solidifies
- Frequent last-minute changes with no good notification system

### Parent State During Coordination
- **Thinking:** "I need to figure out the logistics before this becomes a problem."
- **Feeling:** The dull, low-grade work of administration; satisfaction when it comes together
- **Time spent (hypothesis):** 1–4 hours for initial setup; ongoing overhead each week

### What We Need to Validate
- [ ] Is carpool coordination a significant pain point or a minor one?
- [ ] How much of the coordination is between two parents in one household vs. across multiple families?
- [ ] Does the coordination burden scale significantly with the number of kids and activities?
- [ ] What is the most common breakdown in the coordination phase?

---

## Stage 6: Experience
**The activity itself**

*Note: Joyful is not present during the activity itself. But what happens here informs what comes next.*

### What Matters for the Product
- Did the activity meet expectations?
- Did the child have a peer connection there?
- Did the parent connect with other parents at pickup?
- Did anything happen that they would want to recommend (or warn against)?

### Parent State During Experience
- **Thinking:** Was this the right choice? Does my kid like this?
- **Feeling:** Hope, observation, mild evaluation

---

## Stage 7: Reflect + Share
**After the activity — the feedback loop**

### Hypothesized Reflection Behavior
- After 1–3 sessions: informal assessment (is my kid excited to go back?)
- Positive experience → tells 1–3 friends proactively
- Negative experience → warns friends who ask
- Rarely: posts a review publicly

### The Sharing Gap
Parents generate enormous amounts of high-quality, trust-rich recommendation data in private conversations. Almost none of it is captured in a structured way. This is the data flywheel Joyful needs to unlock.

### Parent State at Reflection
- **Thinking:** "Was this worth it? Would I recommend this?"
- **Feeling:** Satisfaction if it worked; regret if it didn't; desire to share if it was exceptional

### What We Need to Validate
- [ ] How proactively do parents share recommendations after a good experience?
- [ ] What prompts them to share vs. keep it to themselves?
- [ ] What's the threshold for a recommendation — "it was fine" vs. "I'd tell every parent I know"?
- [ ] Is there an existing behavior of capturing recommendations somewhere (notes app, saved messages, etc.)?

## The Moments That Matter Most (Hypothesis)

Based on this journey, here are the moments where Joyful can deliver the most value:

| Moment | Joyful Intervention | Job Being Done |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger fires** | Proactive suggestion arrives before parent even starts searching | Trusted Discovery |
| **Search begins** | First screen shows trusted network recommendations, ranked and filtered | Trusted Discovery |
| **Evaluation** | "3 parents you know recommend this for a 7-year-old who loves building" | Trusted Discovery + Social Belonging |
| **Decision made** | One-tap to add to calendar + notify co-parent | Frictionless Coordination |
| **Carpool needed** | "2 families in your network are also going on Tuesdays" | Frictionless Coordination + Social Belonging |
| **Post-experience** | "How was it? Would you recommend it?" prompt at right time | Data flywheel |

## How This Map Changes After Research

After 50 interviews, revisit every stage and answer:
- Was our hypothesis about what parents do at this stage accurate?
- Where were we most wrong?
- What stage has more pain than we thought?
- What stage has less pain than we thought?
- Are there stages we missed entirely?

The updated journey map becomes the foundation for your feature priority decisions.
