# Jobs to Be Done: Hypothesis Canvas

This document captures our *current hypotheses* about what jobs parents are hiring Joyful to do. These are not facts — they are informed guesses that customer discovery will validate, refine, or invalidate.

For each hypothesis, note your confidence level before research and update it after.

---

## What Is a Job to Be Done?

A job is the progress a person is trying to make in a specific circumstance. It has three dimensions:

- **Functional job**: The practical task they're trying to accomplish
- **Emotional job**: How they want to feel while doing it (or as a result)
- **Social job**: How they want to be perceived by others

The full JTBD statement follows this form:
> *When [situation], I want to [motivation / job], so I can [expected outcome].*

Products win when they do the job better than the current alternatives — not when they have more features.

## Hypothesized Jobs (Pre-Research)

---

### Job 1: Trusted Discovery
**The job:** Find new, age-appropriate activities for my child without wasting time on options that won't fit us.

**Full statement:**
> *When I sense my child is ready for something new, or a new season is starting, I want to get recommendations from parents whose taste and values I trust, so I can feel confident the activity is worth our time and money before we commit.*

**Functional:** Find relevant, filtered activity options quickly.
**Emotional:** Feel confident and competent as a parent, not overwhelmed by choice paralysis.
**Social:** Be seen (by yourself and others) as an intentional parent who makes thoughtful choices.

**Current alternatives (what parents use today):**
- Texting 2–3 parents they trust directly
- Asking in Lamorinda Moms Facebook group (noisy, low filter)
- Google search (too broad, no trust signal)
- Yelp (strangers, often stale)
- Word of mouth at school pickup
- School newsletter or parent email list

**Why current alternatives fall short (hypothesis):**
- Texting parents is high quality but doesn't scale; you can only ask so many people
- Facebook groups have too much noise and too little trust filtering
- Google/Yelp lack the social trust layer entirely
- Word of mouth is serendipitous — you have to happen to be in the right conversation

**What we need to learn:**
- [ ] How often does the discovery cycle happen? (Weekly? Monthly? Seasonally?)
- [ ] Who are the 1–3 people parents actually call/text for recommendations? What makes them trusted?
- [ ] How often do recommendations from trusted parents not work out? (Tests the trust assumption)
- [ ] Is "confidence" the right emotional job, or is it something else — relief? excitement? validation?
- [ ] Does the pain of bad discovery (wasted money, disappointed kid) outweigh the friction of current process?

**Confidence pre-research:** High — this is the most commonly cited pain in early conversations.

### Job 2: Frictionless Coordination
**The job:** Turn a discovery ("we should do this") into an action ("we're signed up and going") without it becoming a project.

**Full statement:**
> *When we've decided on an activity, I want to handle the logistics — signing up, scheduling, coordinating with my co-parent or other families — without it requiring a separate time investment, so the activity actually happens instead of getting lost in good intentions.*

**Functional:** Complete the logistics chain from decision to enrolled.
**Emotional:** Feel like I have things handled; avoid the low-level anxiety of open loops.
**Social:** Not be the parent who always says "we should do that" and never follows through.

**Current alternatives:**
- Manual calendar management (Google Calendar, Apple Calendar)
- Text chains with co-parent and other parents
- Activity provider website (often clunky, requires creating an account per provider)
- Paper flyers that get buried in backpacks

**Why current alternatives fall short (hypothesis):**
- No single place that connects discovery → scheduling → coordination
- Signing up for 5 different activities means 5 different login accounts
- Co-parent coordination often means double-entering things
- No smart conflict detection across kids' activities and family schedule

**What we need to learn:**
- [ ] How much time does the post-discovery coordination actually take? Is it truly painful or just mildly annoying?
- [ ] Where does the handoff break down most often? (Between discovery and decision? Decision and signup? Signup and calendar?)
- [ ] Do parents manage one family calendar together, or does each parent have their own?
- [ ] How much of the coordination is with a co-parent vs. with other families?
- [ ] What does "follow through" failure look like? (Activity never booked, activity booked but kid doesn't go, kid drops out early?)

**Confidence pre-research:** Medium — we've heard this but haven't quantified the magnitude.

### Job 3: Social Belonging Through Shared Experience
**The job:** Make sure my child (and me) have community connections through the activities we choose.

**Full statement:**
> *When choosing activities for my child, I want to know which families we know are also doing similar things, so my child has friends in the activity and I have parents I know at pickup — making the whole experience more enjoyable for both of us.*

**Functional:** See social/network overlap before committing to an activity.
**Emotional:** Feel connected, not isolated; reduce the anxiety of a child entering a new environment alone.
**Social:** Be part of the community fabric, not on the outside of it.

**Current alternatives:**
- Asking specific friends directly ("Is Sophie doing swim team this year?")
- Facebook group posts
- Finding out at the first session (too late to influence the decision)

**Why current alternatives fall short (hypothesis):**
- There's no proactive signal — you find out about overlap reactively or by chance
- The information exists but is scattered across individual conversations

**What we need to learn:**
- [ ] How much does social overlap actually influence activity decisions? (Significant factor or nice-to-have?)
- [ ] Is the driver more "my child will know kids there" or "I will know parents there"?
- [ ] Does this vary significantly by child age? (More important for younger kids? Less for teens?)
- [ ] Have parents ever chosen or avoided an activity based on who else was doing it?

**Confidence pre-research:** Medium — this comes up in conversation but may be secondary to discovery.

### Job 4: Peace of Mind About the Whole System
**The job:** Feel like the logistics layer of parenting is under control so I can be present with my kids.

**Full statement:**
> *When the calendar is full and the coordination overhead is high, I want to feel like I have a handle on everything — nothing is falling through the cracks, no one is missing anything — so I can stop managing and start being present.*

**Functional:** Eliminate open loops and uncertainty in the family schedule.
**Emotional:** Replace low-grade anxiety with calm confidence.
**Social:** Be the parent who "has it together" — reliable, present, not frantic.

**Current alternatives:**
- Heavy use of Google Calendar with color-coding per child
- Shared family calendar apps (Cozi, etc.)
- Extensive group chats with other parents
- Mental load (the work of remembering everything)

**Why current alternatives fall short (hypothesis):**
- Tools exist for scheduling but not for the *intelligence* layer (is this the right activity? is there a conflict? do we need a carpool?)
- The mental load is distributed unevenly — often falls disproportionately on one parent
- No tool helps you *anticipate* problems before they happen

**What we need to learn:**
- [ ] How significant is the mental load of activity management compared to the discovery problem?
- [ ] Who carries more of the mental load in two-parent households? Does this create relationship tension?
- [ ] What does "anxiety" look like in practice — is it Sunday night worry, or daily cognitive overhead?
- [ ] Is this a job parents want a tool to do, or do they want to *not* have to think about it at all? (Important for AI concierge framing)

**Confidence pre-research:** Medium-high — "mental load" language comes up frequently in early conversations.

### Job 5: Intentional Development (Wonder, Not Logistics)
**The job:** Make deliberate, developmental choices for my child — not just manage the logistics of activities.

**Full statement:**
> *When I'm thinking about what activities to put my child in, I want to be guided by what will genuinely help them grow — not just what's convenient or what other families are doing — so I feel like an intentional parent who is helping my child become who they're meant to be.*

**Functional:** Access relevant developmental guidance contextualized to my specific child.
**Emotional:** Feel intentional, not reactive; proud rather than guilty.
**Social:** Be a thoughtful parent, not one who's just filling time.

**Current alternatives:**
- Pediatrician advice (episodic, not activity-specific)
- Parenting books / podcasts (general, not personalized)
- Their own intuition
- Recommendations from teachers or coaches

**Why current alternatives fall short (hypothesis):**
- Developmental guidance is generic; it doesn't know your child
- The gap between "what's good developmentally" and "what's available near me" is never bridged
- Parents feel this job but may not articulate it directly in interviews — it's beneath the surface

**What we need to learn:**
- [ ] Do Lafayette parents explicitly think about developmental stages when choosing activities, or is it more intuitive?
- [ ] Is this a job they want help with, or does it feel too personal / they want to own it themselves?
- [ ] Does developmental guidance feel empowering or preachy? (Important for product tone)
- [ ] Is this more important for younger children (4–8) than older (9–14)?

**Confidence pre-research:** Low-medium — this is the Phase 4 vision, but we need to learn whether it belongs in the MVP conversation or not.

## Job Priority Hypothesis (Pre-Research)

Based on early conversations and domain intuition, here is our current bet on relative priority:

```
Highest Pain / Most Urgent
│
│  1. Trusted Discovery        ← Immediate, frequent, well-articulated pain
│  2. Frictionless Coordination ← Follows directly from discovery; same episode
│  3. Social Belonging          ← Meaningful but may be secondary motivator
│  4. Peace of Mind             ← Real but diffuse; harder to build a product around
│  5. Intentional Development   ← Aspirational; longer-term product layer
│
Lowest Pain / Most Latent
```

**This ranking will change.** After 50 interviews, update it here with evidence.

## Hypotheses to Explicitly Test

These are the specific beliefs we need to confirm or kill before committing to building.

| # | Hypothesis | How to Test | Kill Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Parents trust network recommendations significantly more than stranger reviews | Ask in interviews; observe whether they describe trusted people unprompted | If < 70% mention trusted network as primary source |
| H2 | Discovery is the highest-friction job, not coordination | Compare time/effort estimates for each in interviews | If coordination frustration is described more intensely than discovery |
| H3 | The trigger for discovery is seasonal / episodic, not continuous | Ask when and how often they look for new activities | If continuous searching is common, product design changes significantly |
| H4 | Social overlap (who else is going) is a meaningful input to the decision | Ask directly and listen for unprompted mentions | If < 30% mention social overlap as a factor |
| H5 | Parents will share their contact list / network to power recommendations | Ask directly in concept reaction | If majority express discomfort, the onboarding approach must change |
| H6 | Parents in Lafayette/Lamorinda have a distinct community identity that makes local trust networks more valuable | Listen for Lamorinda-specific language and references | If they don't reference local community identity, expansion market choice may need rethinking |
| H7 | There is willingness to pay $10–15/month for this job done well | Ask directly at end of concept reaction | If < 30% express genuine (not polite) interest in paying |
