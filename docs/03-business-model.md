# Business Model: How Joyful Makes Money

The goal: build a sustainable business that aligns your revenue model with your mission. You win when families win.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Parents pay for value, not for data**: You will never sell user data or depend on advertising. This is a brand commitment and a business decision.
2. **The free tier grows the network**: Discovery and social features stay free because network density drives value. Monetize the individual utility features.
3. **Align revenue with activity**: When Joyful helps a family find and book an activity, you earn a piece of that. You're directly incentivized to make discovery and booking work.
4. **B2C first, B2B second**: Nail the consumer product first. Enterprise/B2B opportunities (schools, corporate benefits) come after you have an audience worth selling to.

---

## Revenue Streams by Phase

### Phase 1: Free (Months 1–5)
**No revenue. That's intentional.**

In Phase 1, your only goal is adoption and NPS. Charging too early:
- Slows down the network growth that makes discovery valuable
- Adds friction to the critical viral loop (parent invites parent)
- Forces you to justify price before you've proven value

Build a waitlist. Offer "founding family" status to early adopters. Promise them the best price ever and a seat at the table on product decisions.

### Phase 2 Introduction: Freemium SaaS (Months 5+)

**Free Tier (always free)**
- Activity discovery feed
- Up to 20 trust network connections
- Basic activity ratings
- "Who else is going?" coordination
- Monthly limit: 20 AI-powered searches

**Joyful Premium — $12/month or $99/year**
- Unlimited AI-powered discovery and search
- Unlimited network connections
- Calendar sync and scheduling
- Activity booking (Phase 2)
- Smart reminders and prep checklists
- Priority access to new features

**Joyful Family Plan — $19/month or $159/year**
- Everything in Premium
- Up to 4 co-parent/caregiver access
- Carpool coordination (Phase 3)
- Developmental insights (Phase 4)
- Family graph features (Phase 5)

### Phase 2+: Marketplace Commission
**10–15% on bookings made through Joyful**

When a parent books a pottery class through Joyful's in-app booking:
- Class costs $180
- Joyful takes 10–15% = $18–27
- Activity provider gets the rest

This aligns your incentive with the parent's outcome. You only earn when they find and use a great activity.

**Why providers will work with you:**
- You send them qualified leads (parents who are actively looking, filtered for age-appropriateness)
- You reduce their marketing cost (they don't have to be on every platform)
- You provide trusted social proof (endorsements from real parent networks)
- You handle booking logistics

**Provider acquisition strategy:**
- Start with 20–30 providers in your launch city who want growth
- Offer 0% commission for the first 3 months (proven value first)
- Build a simple provider portal: manage availability, see bookings, see reviews
- Providers pay for premium placement once you have traffic

### Phase 3+: Provider Premium Listings
**$99–$299/month per provider location**

Beyond commission, providers can pay for:
- Featured placement in discovery feed
- "Verified" badge with enhanced trust signals
- Analytics dashboard: how many Joyful families viewed/saved/booked
- Direct messaging to interested families (with permission)

### Phase 4+: Employer Benefits
**$15–25 per employee per month**

Large employers (tech companies, hospitals, law firms) offer childcare/family benefits to retain working parents. Joyful Premium bundled as an employee benefit is:
- High perceived value for employees ($144–$228/year)
- Easy ROI story for HR: reduces parenting-related distraction and turnover
- Acquisition channel for hundreds/thousands of families at once

**Target employers:** Companies with > 200 employees, 40%+ workforce with kids, strong employee benefits culture. Tech companies are the obvious first target.

## Unit Economics (What to Target)

### For a Premium Subscriber
| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Monthly price | $12 |
| Annual price | $99 |
| Monthly AI cost per user | ~$0.50–$2.00 |
| Monthly infrastructure cost per user | ~$0.10 |
| Gross margin (annual sub) | ~78–82% |

### For a Marketplace Booking
| Metric | Example |
|---|---|
| Average booking value | $150 |
| Commission rate | 12% |
| Revenue per booking | $18 |
| Bookings per active family/month | 0.5 |
| Marketplace revenue per family/month | $9 |

### Combined LTV Model
A family that subscribes to Premium AND makes 1 booking/month:
- Subscription: $8.25/month (annualized)
- Booking commission: $9/month
- **Total: ~$17.25/month, ~$207/year**

If CAC (cost to acquire) is $30–50 (reasonable for a social product with good virality), payback period is **2–3 months**. Excellent unit economics.

## Virality and CAC

Your most important growth lever is virality. The trust graph IS your growth loop.

**The loop:**
1. Parent joins (invite from friend or organic discovery)
2. Parent invites friends to build their trust network
3. Each friend joins, invites their friends
4. Network density increases → recommendation quality improves → NPS increases → more referrals

**Measuring virality:**
- **K-factor**: Average number of new users each existing user brings in
- Target K-factor: > 0.5 in Phase 1, > 0.8 by Phase 2
- A K-factor > 1.0 means you grow without paid acquisition

**Paid acquisition channels (Phase 2+):**
- Instagram / TikTok (parenting content drives discovery)
- Google ("kids activities [city]" search intent is high)
- Parenting newsletters and podcasts (highly targeted, trusted)
- School parent associations (partner → bulk onboarding)

**Target blended CAC**: < $30 including paid and organic

## What to Avoid

**Advertising-based model**: Puts you in conflict with your users. Parents will not trust a product that shows them ads for kids' services.

**Freemium with too-tight free tier**: If the free tier is too limited, the network won't grow and your paid tier has nothing to convert into.

**Charging too early**: NPS < 50 means you haven't found product-market fit. Don't add payment friction before you've earned it.

**Overcomplicating the pricing**: One free tier, one consumer paid tier, one family paid tier. That's it for Phase 1–2. Add complexity only when your current structure limits growth.
