# Team & Hiring Guide

Who you hire in the first 12 months will determine the trajectory of the company more than almost anything else. These early teammates set the culture, define the quality bar, and become the people you'll spend most of your waking hours with.

---

## The Founding Team Philosophy

**Hire for mission fit first, skill second.**

At this stage, skills can be learned and scope can be adjusted. But someone who doesn't deeply care about helping parents raise thriving kids — who doesn't feel the problem viscerally — will make a hundred small decisions that slowly drift you away from your mission.

**The right first team has:**
- A product leader who is obsessed with the problem (that's you)
- A technical builder who has shipped consumer products
- Someone who can talk to users constantly and turn it into insight

---

## The Ideal Founding Team Configuration

### You: Founder + Chief Product Officer
**What you own:**
- Vision and product strategy
- Customer discovery and user research
- Roadmap prioritization
- Community building and early GTM
- Fundraising
- Content and brand voice
- Hiring

**What you need to get better at:**
- Writing precise, unambiguous product specs (this is a skill — practice it)
- Reading and interpreting your analytics without an analyst
- Making fast decisions with incomplete information (the cost of indecision is high at a startup)
- Saying no — to features, to partnerships, to distractions

### Hire #1: Technical Co-Founder or Lead Engineer
**The most important hire you'll make**

This person will make hundreds of consequential technical decisions in the first 6 months. They need to be both technically strong and genuinely excited about your mission.

**What to look for:**
- Has built and shipped at least 2 consumer mobile or web products
- Has worked with AI/LLM APIs before (or has clear curiosity and demonstrated learning speed)
- Can work full-stack: database design, API development, AND frontend
- Has opinions about user experience, not just technical architecture
- Communicates clearly with non-technical people
- Has worked in a startup environment (0→1 experience)

**Title options:** Co-Founder + CTO (for equity-based; best option), VP of Engineering (for salary-based senior hire), Lead Engineer (for a strong individual contributor)

**Compensation:**
- Co-Founder / CTO: 5–15% equity, minimal salary until funded
- VP Engineering: $160–200K + 0.5–2% equity
- Lead Engineer (contract): $120–150/hr or $150–180K FTE + 0.25–1% equity

**Where to find them:**
- Your existing network (best source)
- YC co-founder matching (yc.com/cofounder-matching)
- LinkedIn (search: "full stack engineer" + "consumer apps" + city)
- Lunchclub.com
- Hacker News "Who wants to be hired" threads
- Engineering communities: Locally Optimistic Slack, various Discord communities

### Hire #2: Designer (Part-time or Contract, Months 2–5)
Before you have revenue, you can't afford a full-time designer. But you can't afford to launch something that looks like it was designed by engineers.

**What to look for:**
- Has designed consumer mobile apps (not just enterprise software)
- Has opinions about interaction design, not just visual design
- Understands the emotion you're trying to create (warmth, trust, simplicity)
- Can move fast and iterate (startup designers ship, then refine)
- Shows work that looks *calm and trustworthy*, not flashy

**Compensation:**
- Contract: $75–120/hr, ~20 hrs/week
- Full-time (Months 5+): $120–150K + 0.25–0.75% equity

**Where to find them:**
- Contra.com (freelance designer platform)
- Dribbble (review portfolios first, then reach out)
- Your network
- Layers.to (design community)

### Hire #3: Community + Growth Manager (Months 3–6)
Your champion parent strategy requires a person whose full-time job is community building. In Phase 1, this might be you. By Month 4–5, if it's working, you need someone dedicated.

**What to look for:**
- Deep connection to the target user (ideally a parent themselves, in your target demographic)
- Has built online or offline communities before
- Writes well and communicates warmth in text
- Is genuinely curious about people — loves talking to users
- Has some content creation skills (can write a newsletter, shoot a quick video)

**This is NOT a traditional marketing hire.** You don't want someone who thinks in campaigns and CAC. You want someone who thinks in relationships and trust.

**Compensation:**
- $75–100K + 0.1–0.3% equity

## Hiring Process for Your First 5 Hires

### The Founder's Interview Rule
For every hire in the first year, you should be the last interview and you should ask two questions:
1. *"Tell me about a time you worked on something you genuinely cared about. How was that different from work you didn't care about?"*
2. *"What do you know about the problem we're solving? Have you experienced it yourself?"*

If their eyes don't light up at some point in the conversation, that's data.

### The Work Sample Test
For every technical hire: ask them to do a small paid project (4–8 hours) before extending an offer. Pay them $200–500 for their time. What you're evaluating:
- Quality of their output
- How they handle ambiguity (do they ask good questions?)
- How they communicate while working
- Whether they think about the user, not just the code

For a designer: give them a specific flow to design (onboarding, or the activity detail screen) and evaluate their process as much as their output.

### Reference Checks (Don't Skip These)
Ask references: *"What did [name] do when they hit a hard blocker they couldn't solve alone?"* and *"Describe a time they had to push back on direction they disagreed with."* The answers to these tell you more than any interview.

## Team Structure at Each Phase

### Phase 0–1 (Months 0–5): 2–3 people
- You (product, GTM, fundraising)
- 1 Lead Engineer
- 1 Part-time Designer

### Phase 1–2 (Months 5–9): 4–5 people
- You
- 1 Lead Engineer
- 1 Full-time Designer
- 1 Community + Growth Manager
- 1 Second Engineer (if needed)

### Phase 2–3 (Months 9–18): 8–12 people
- You
- CTO / VP Engineering
- 2–3 Engineers
- 1 Designer
- 1 Head of Growth
- 1 Community Manager
- 1 Data Analyst
- 1 Operations (bookings, provider relations)

### What to Outsource vs. Hire
| Function | Outsource | Hire |
|---|---|---|
| Legal | Yes (startup law firm) | Never in first 2 years |
| Accounting / Bookkeeping | Yes (Pilot.com) | CFO at Series A |
| PR | Yes (fractional PR, $3–5K/month) | Director of Comms at Series A |
| Content creation | Yes (contractors, early) | When content is a core channel |
| Customer support | You do it first, then: | Hire at 2,000+ families |
| Data / analytics | Yes (PostHog does most of it) | Hire at Series A |

## Fundraising Timeline

### Pre-Seed (Now – Month 6)
**Target: $500K – $1.5M**
**Source: Friends & family, angels, pre-seed funds**

What you're selling: the team, the vision, the problem validation.
What you need: 50+ customer interviews, a compelling deck, a waitlist, and a clear plan for how this money gets you to a fundable Series A.

Pre-seed funds to research: Precursor, Hustle Fund, Backstage Capital, Maven Ventures, specific parenting/edtech focused angels.

### Seed (Month 6–12)
**Target: $2M – $4M**
**Source: Seed-stage VCs, strategic angels**

What you need to show: 500+ active families, NPS > 50, early revenue signal, strong retention (> 50% Month 1 retention), clear evidence of social virality.

### Series A (Month 18–24)
**Target: $8M – $15M**
**Source: Series A VCs**

What you need to show: 10,000+ active families, $75K+ MRR, clear path to $1M ARR, evidence that the network effects are real, multi-city expansion playbook.

## A Note on Fundraising as a Non-Technical Founder

VCs will ask: *"Why you?"* Be ready with your answer.

Your answer is not just your background. It's:
1. **Deep user empathy**: You are the target user. You understand this problem better than a 25-year-old engineer who doesn't have kids.
2. **Product leadership experience**: You have shipped products before. You know how to turn user needs into features.
3. **Network and distribution**: You have relationships that give you access to early communities.
4. **Clarity of vision**: You have thought deeply about the long arc of this company and can articulate it.

The technical gap is real — be honest about it. *"My co-founder handles the technical architecture. My job is to make sure we're building the right thing and that parents fall in love with it."* Then make sure your co-founder is in the room and impressive.
